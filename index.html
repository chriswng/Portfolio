My Practice
01
Data integrity precedes narrative.
Every published net zero commitment is a claim. The credibility of that claim depends on what is behind it: a defensible, independently verifiable emissions inventory scoped and documented before the narrative is drafted, so the audit trail exists whether or not external scrutiny follows.
02
Reusable infrastructure compounds value.
Advisory that lives in a consultant's head has a short shelf life. I build models, templates, and processes designed for internal operation, so the organisation retains the methodology, can answer assurance questions, and can run the reporting cycle the following year without calling for external support.
03
Ambition grounded in evidence, with gaps named.
The science sets the pace, not the strategy. Pathway models need lever assumptions grounded in published data, with estimation uncertainty disclosed alongside the figures. Where carbon credits are part of the plan, they address residual hard-to-abate emissions and are not a substitute for near-term reduction commitments.

Results
01
GRESB Infrastructure Assessment: 35-Point Improvement
A major Australian energy distributor was consistently underperforming on the GRESB Infrastructure Assessment relative to sector peers. Data collection was inconsistent, methodology documentation was insufficient, and the submission process lacked ownership. The score gap reflected a structural problem, not a performance one. I led the full cycle from gap analysis through to submission: mapped each indicator against available data, identified the highest-impact gaps, redesigned the data collection process to close them, and coordinated targeted disclosure uplift across the management, performance, and stakeholder engagement components. The methodology was documented to make the process transferable to internal staff.
Outcome: 35-percentage-point improvement in year one. Submission handed back as a repeatable, internally operable process.
02
Net Zero Pathway Model: Board-Level Presentation
A large infrastructure services group required a net zero pathway model capable of presenting credible scenario analysis to board level, with lever assumptions traceable to published Australian sources and outputs navigable by non-specialists. The existing approach used static spreadsheets with no scenario toggle capability and no version control. I built a toggle-based decarbonisation scenario model incorporating DCCEEW 2025 grid emission factor projections, NVES Act 2024 fleet trajectories, and pathway curves filtered by business segment. Lever assumptions were sourced from published government and research data. The model was structured for internal operation: documented methodology and no dependency on the analyst who built it.
Outcome: Presented to the board by senior leadership as part of the Group's climate strategy. Became the internal reference tool for tracking business unit abatement progress against the net zero pathway.
03
NSW Treasury Net Zero Government Operations Policy
The NSW Treasury was developing its Net Zero Government Operations Policy and required a technically sound methodology framework for agency-level emissions reporting across the NSW Government. The challenge was designing a boundary definition approach consistent with GHG Protocol principles, operable by agencies with varying data capability, and defensible under public scrutiny. I contributed technical input covering emissions boundary definition and reporting methodology for the whole-of-government framework: operational control versus equity share boundary treatment for outsourced government functions, data tier hierarchy for agencies with limited primary data, and alignment with NGER reporting requirements.
Outcome: Input incorporated into the policy framework applied across NSW Government agencies for agency-level net zero reporting.

A structured three-stage methodology for moving from emissions data to a sequenced decarbonisation roadmap with CAPEX implications. Applied across infrastructure, property, and government sector engagements.

Stage 01
Establish Baseline & Constraints
Objective: Understand starting point and limits
  • Confirm Scope 1–3 boundaries, data confidence, and material gaps
  • Identify constraints: regulatory, commercial, operational
  • Map key stakeholders: portfolio managers, facility managers, procurement
  • Understand existing decarbonisation efforts and commitments
Potential data gaps at this stage: capital goods, business travel, waste in operations – flagged for proxy methodology or exclusion with rationale.
Stage 02
Identify & Quantify Mitigation Options
Objective: Generate and evaluate options
  • Develop long-list of initiatives across Scope 1–3
  • Quantify abatement, cost, and timing for each option
  • Identify initiatives to avoid, reduce, and replace emissions
  • Cover operations, design, supply chain, and asset use-phase
Acknowledge ongoing decarbonisation scenarios: national grid trajectory, material and technology changes, policy pipeline.
Stage 03
Screen & Build the Roadmap
Objective: Narrow options into a sequenced plan
  • Short-list options with stakeholders using MCA (see next tab)
  • Build sequenced roadmap with CAPEX implications by year
  • Set interim targets aligned to SBTi or AASB S2 scenarios
  • Define governance, ownership, and monitoring cadence
Output: a pathway model where scenario toggles show how lever choices affect the trajectory – gross vs. net, by scope and business unit.

A multi-criteria analysis framework for prioritising decarbonisation initiatives. Applied to move from a long-list of options to a sequenced roadmap with CAPEX implications. Weighting factors are agreed with stakeholders following engagement – the framework structures the decision, it does not make it.

Impact (Carbon)
Metric: total tCO₂-e reduction to 2050
Will the option deliver significant carbon reductions toward net zero targets? Primary quantitative screen.
Cost Effectiveness
Metric: Net Present Cost / tCO₂-e avoided
Is the initiative cost-effective in achieving carbon reductions? Levelised cost of abatement used to identify the efficiency frontier before CAPEX commitments.
Readiness
Metric: technology and commercial maturity
Is the option technically and commercially ready to deploy at scale? What time horizon does it become feasible? Informs sequencing.
Ability to Influence
Metric: organisational control vs. external dependency
Does the organisation have direct ability to act, or is collaboration and third-party decisions required? Determines ownership model.
Risks & Constraints
Metric: safety, regulatory, reputational exposure
Significant safety, licensing, regulatory, or community risks? Go/no-go screen for initiatives with material downside exposure.
Co-benefits
Metric: value beyond carbon savings
Associated environmental, community, or strategic value beyond carbon savings. Includes resilience, regulatory positioning, and commercial differentiation.

Embodied carbon is front-loaded
65% of the lifecycle carbon footprint is locked in before practical completion. Procurement and specification decisions – not operational management – determine the outcome.
Operational carbon is addressable
All-electric design paired with a grid decarbonisation trajectory can drive use-phase Scope 2 emissions to near-zero by the mid-2030s. This is the primary lever for buildings with long asset lives.
Module D credits are real, but not a substitute
Beyond-boundary credits from reuse, recycling, and energy recovery (Module D) are valid in whole-life assessments. They should not be used to offset A1–A5 hotspots.

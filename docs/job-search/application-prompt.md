### What to produce

For each role, in this order:

**A. The CV.** One document per role, two pages maximum, reordered and reweighted for
that role. Same underlying facts every time, never invented, but the bullets selected
and sequenced differently so the most relevant evidence sits in the top third of page
one. Include a three-line summary at the top written for that specific employer.
Quantify wherever the record quantifies; never add a number that is not above.

**B. The cover letter.** One page, no more than 400 words, four or five paragraphs.
Open with the specific reason this role and not a generic statement of enthusiasm.
Middle paragraphs each carry one concrete piece of evidence tied to something the
employer actually asked for. Address the main gap once, briefly, in a way that shows
he knows what the role needs. Close with a plain, confident line about next steps.

**C. A short application note** for Chris, not for the employer: which gap will get
probed at interview, and the single strongest thing to prepare.

### How to write

- Australian English throughout.
- No em dashes anywhere. No en dashes as clause separators in prose.
- Active voice. Plain, direct sentences.
- Write in Chris's voice: precise, evidence-led, unshowy. He describes what he built
  and what it changed, and lets the numbers do the persuading. He does not use words
  like passionate, leverage, synergy, or drive impact.
- No superlatives he cannot support and no claim that is not in the record above.
  If a requirement genuinely is not met, either leave it alone or address it
  honestly. Never imply experience he lacks.
- Titles and headings never end in a full stop.
- Every claim must trace to a specific line in the record above. If you find yourself
  reaching for a fact that is not there, stop and ask rather than filling the gap.

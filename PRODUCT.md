# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Hiring leaders, founders, and heads of sustainability in fashion, consumer
goods, and technology who are evaluating Christopher Wang for senior
sustainability roles or advisory engagements. They arrive from LinkedIn, a
referral, or a direct share, usually with a specific question in mind: can this
person build emissions reporting infrastructure that survives external
assurance and stand up a credible decarbonisation pathway. They read closely,
skim little, and judge rigour by how the work is presented as much as by the
claims themselves.

## Product Purpose

The portfolio is the proof, not a summary of it. It exists to show that Chris
turns emissions data into governed reporting infrastructure and defensible
decarbonisation pathways, and that the same care shows up in how the work is
built. Success is a reader who finishes a section trusting the methodology, and
who reaches out because the site made the standard of the work legible without
overselling it. The Life Footprint dashboard and its basis of preparation carry
that further: they let a visitor exercise the modelling rigour directly instead
of reading about it.

## Brand Personality

Measured, rigorous, editorial. The voice is a practitioner who documents
assumptions and shows the working, never a marketer reaching for a superlative.
Every figure is sourced, every model traceable. The tone is calm and precise,
confident because the evidence is there, not because the copy insists on it.

Three-word personality: **rigorous, editorial, understated**.

## Anti-references

The portfolio must not read like the generic templates it is trying to rise
above. Specifically, avoid:

- **Consultant-deck clichés**: stock hero imagery, vague impact statements, big
  round numbers with no source, gradient-on-dark hero panels.
- **Sustainability greenwash aesthetics**: leaf motifs, earnest earth-tone
  gradients, unsupported percentage claims, feel-good abstraction.
- **Hedging or hype**: neither "might help you save the planet" nor "10x your
  ESG score". State what was done, for whom, with what result.
- **Motion for its own sake**: animation earns its place or it does not ship,
  and it always respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the evidence.** Presentation quality is itself a claim about
   the quality of the work. Anything sloppy undercuts the argument.
2. **Every figure is sourced.** Numbers carry their context and provenance. The
   footprint model shows its basis of preparation so a reader can audit it.
3. **Editorial over marketing.** This reads like a considered publication, with
   long-form clarity, deliberate typography, and room to breathe, not a SaaS
   landing page.
4. **Restraint with confidence.** Neutrals lean toward the matcha brand hue at
   low chroma on purpose. Nothing decorative arrives without a job to do.
5. **Written in Chris's voice.** Australian English, active voice, no em
   dashes, no filler. The copy sounds like the person behind the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Baseline: WCAG 2.1 AA on every page. Key commitments:
- Colour contrast held to the original lightness values and verified, not
  eyeballed.
- Skip link first, `aria-current` on the active nav link, visible focus states,
  full keyboard navigation.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected for every animation; canvas and WebGL
  loops pause off-screen and clean up on unmount.
- Decorative canvases marked `aria-hidden`; purely visual motion such as the
  cycling role text carries a static `.sr-only` label for assistive tech.
- Semantic HTML first, ARIA as a supplement, never as a workaround.
